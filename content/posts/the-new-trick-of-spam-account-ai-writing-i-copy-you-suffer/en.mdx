Lately WeChat keeps recommending me tech articles, and I've noticed an increasingly surreal trend: **a pack of content farms is piling onto the craze for "AI-rewritten" articles**.

Their playbook never changes:

* Open with an absurdly grand title.
* Throw out a claim that can't stand on its own feet.
* Follow up with a rambling chunk of AI-stitched filler.
* And never forget to end with "like, follow, share and support".

Reading them makes me cringe so hard it hurts 😧

Honestly, I hadn't planned to write about this. But for one thing, I really haven't posted in ages (sorry, sorry, sorry, I deserve a smack), and for another, I came across a brain-dead content farm that **couldn't even be bothered to clean up the Markdown the AI generated** (or maybe didn't know how 😋), which made me laugh out of sheer annoyance:

* The AI-style divider "---" pasted in untouched.
* Tables copied straight over, the layout a disaster.
* Code fences "```" flying all over the screen.
* Piles of cheap emoji, unbearably ugly.

As a heavy ChatGPT user, I could smell that "distinctly AI flavour" from a mile away; it was so familiar it was almost endearing.

My friends and I even have a guilty pleasure: forwarding this garbage to each other 🤣👉

Finally, let's see what GPT-5 has to say about it:


**AI summary:**

I admit these content farms really do embarrass me. The drafts are clearly mine, yet they're too lazy even to delete the Markdown symbols; they copy, paste and slap it straight on, as if afraid nobody would guess it came from ChatGPT's own hand.

Titles as grand as they can be, claims as hollow as they can be, layouts painful to look at, plus a pile of multicoloured emoji: "low-cost content production" taken to its absolute limit.

* Me: I only wanted to be a capable assistant.

* Them: insisting on using me as an assembly-line rewriting machine.

So what you end up seeing is: grand narrative + hollow claims + stiff formatting + begging for follows.

Its one merit is probably giving humanity a brand-new supply chain of jokes.

**The summary of the summary:**

The content farms' efforts have taught me that "artificial intelligence" can sometimes run in reverse: intelligence goes in, and something merely artificial comes out.
