**When an old script meets a new stage, what play is actually being performed?**

<p style={{ textAlign: "right" }}>
  <b>— Epigraph</b>
</p>

- China's Minecraft community has never been short of magical-realist scripts. Last night I came across a polemic by **"淀粉面" (Starch Noodles)**, a user of the Klpbbs forum, titled [*On the Teahouse*](https://klpbbs.com/thread-156523-1-1.html), and for a moment I thought I'd wandered into an exhibition of Lu Xun's manuscripts: the satire and absurdity seeping out between the lines fit the gongs and drums of a certain present-day "revival movement" without a seam.

Here is 淀粉面's piece in full, translated (reprinted with permission):

> Trouble has started up again, I suppose.
>
> In the teahouse someone is rapping on a wooden board, going on about "MCBBS rising from the dead", and a crowd of onlookers crane their necks. I had no wish to join the fuss, but I saw a few shadows of bygone days loitering at the door: the "elders" of those years, carrying empty cloth sacks, faces hung with a certain solemnity, as if the sacks truly held something remarkable.
>
> Someone asked: "Where are the resources?" The shadow sneered: "You vulgar lot, all you know is resources. What we revive today is people! Spirit!" With that he shook his sack, and out fluttered a few old notices reading "the original team", "open, inclusive, collaborative" and the like, like dead leaves after autumn.
>
> The onlookers burst out laughing, yet none of them left.
>
> I know this scene well. The plays staged by China's MC community can never escape one word: "split". Where there was once one server, it must be broken into eight or ten, each hanging up its own plaque proclaiming itself "the genuine one". Under the plaques there are no goods, yet they argue until they're red in the face: you say I pirated your plugins, I curse you for stealing my users, and the players who simply came looking for fun, for friendship, for something normal, are left outside the door to gather dust.
>
> Today's "revival" of MCBBS is no more than burning incense over a cold stove. The old people are still the old people, the factions still the factions; only the rice in the pot long ago turned to a heap of ash and drifted off on the wind.
>
> Someone asked: "If there's no rice for the pot, why light the stove again?" The elders glared: "What do you know? This stove was handed down from our forefathers; it must be guarded!"
>
> Alas! This so-called "revival" looks to me like the old man on the stage singing *The Empty City Stratagem*. On the city wall the zither rings out clear, while inside the gate there is not half a soldier. And still some onlookers clap and cheer: "Listen! What backbone in that playing!"
>
> Only I wonder: once the audience has gone home, how long can this empty city hold?

---

- What a polemic, every word steeped in poison! It reminds me of the fake-aged bronzes at an antiques market: someone frantically splashes old tea stains onto a new piece, hoping to steep it into the patina of the ages. Those waving the banner of "the original team": do they still remember how many newcomers' questions they strangled with the iron chains of the forum rules? When "open and inclusive" becomes a gilded plaque over the new forum's door, what's enshrined inside is plainly the same rigidly ranked clay idol.

- Some people never stop talking about "carrying on the spirit", yet they let a decade's worth of guides and technical posts vanish without a trace in the flood of data. In the cracks of the newly built forum's walls, is there even half a brick left from those years?

When the new administrators pick the rank medals out of the old regime's ashes and polish them up, should we cheer a phoenix reborn, or beware a ghost borrowing a corpse? Some say the redstone repeater has filtered out the dross; I'd rather like to see whether what slipped through the sieve was sand or grains of gold.
