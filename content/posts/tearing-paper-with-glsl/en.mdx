The drawing sheet you land on is really 170 shards. At rest they fit perfectly; scroll, and they split open outward from QwQ's face.

## One draw call

Every shard is merged into a single `BufferGeometry`. Besides position and normal, each vertex carries:

- `aCenter` — the centre of its shard
- `aRand` — a random triple for spin axis and flight distance
- `aDelay` — how far it is from the tear, 0 to 1

The vertex shader subtracts its delay from a global `uBreak` to know where in the motion it is:

```glsl
float local = uBreak - aDelay * 0.6;
if (local > 0.0) {
  float t = clamp((local - 0.06) / 0.5, 0.0, 1.0);
  mat3 R = rotateAxis(axis, t * t * (2.5 + aRand.x * 5.0));
  pos = aCenter + R * (pos - aCenter);
  pos += away * t * t * (16.0 + aRand.y * 18.0);
}
```

## That `if` matters

Without it, the resting sheet was covered in random black specks: the float round-trip in `aCenter + (pos - aCenter)` nudged shared vertices half a pixel apart.

> If it isn't moving, don't touch it.

One more trap: shard walls whose rims sit exactly on the face edges z-fight the faces, and the outline pass inks them as creases. Shrinking the walls by 15% fixes it.
