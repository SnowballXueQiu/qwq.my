最近我在做完形填空时，偶然遇到了一篇有意思的文章，读到最后才发现竟然是一篇脱口秀，尤其是它令人 "眼前一黑" 的 **callback** 结尾。🤣👉

## 正文

> My father was born in a small town in the US. He wasn’t sure what he wanted from life, but something told him to get out and begin a new adventure. He began that adventure traveling to cities in the US before going on to Australia, New Zealand and the Philippines. He took my mother and us three daughters with him and went wherever the road took him.  
>  
> It’s easy to feel lonely when you’re on the road. We made lots of new friends on our trip — most of them are mechanics, since we often spent hours in repair shops. But that was a way much better than sitting by the roadside while waiting for the engine to cool off when it was 40°C outside.  
>  
> Getting along well sometimes seemed impossible. There were always a lot of argument, especially among us back-seat passengers about who had to sit in the middle. But even if it was hard, we learned a lot about tolerance.  
>  
> When we were traveling in the Philippines, we drove to Quezon City one day. It should have been a three-hour drive but was nearly three thanks to bad roads and thick traffic. “Did you put our suitcases in the car?” my father asked my mother after a while. From the back seat, we saw her slowly turn towards my father.  
>  
> “No,” she said. “I thought you did.” That was how a three-hour car trip turned into a nine-hour one, which was mostly spent in silence.  
>  
> On occasions like that, we had to learn to let go of our anger because we were stuck in a rolling box with the same people for the rest of the journey. Even if I sometimes felt like opening the car door and pushing one of my sisters out, I kept my feelings to myself.  
>  
> This is why road trips were like unusual universities to us. We earned our PHDs in how to get along with other people just by traveling in our old car.  
>  
> If we were somehow given a second chance at life, we would do it all over again. <mark>Only this time would I put the suitcases in the car myself.</mark>
