### **When DNA becomes code: could there be an AI program hiding in your cells?**

You know DNA is life's "source code", but you may never have thought that **it runs on the same underlying logic as TikTok's recommendation algorithm!**

No lofty theory today. Let's just chat, in plain words, about how alike two things are: the gene assembly line in your body (the central dogma), and the AI world's magic tool, the recurrent neural network (RNN).

### **1. Your cells are very efficient office workers**
Imagine your cell is a wage slave working overtime around the clock, and DNA is the PowerPoint deck on its desk that never gets finished. But DNA has one quirk: **it only stores things; it never does any work!** So it came up with a slick routine:

1. **Step one: pass the buck (transcription)**
   DNA dumps the whole job on its little helper, mRNA: "Go copy this stretch of code and hand it to the protein department next door!"
   mRNA, baffled: "Boss, this double helix of yours is way too complicated. Can I just copy it out as a single strand?"
   DNA: "Whatever, just don't make me move."

2. **Step two: translation (translation in the literal sense)**
   mRNA carries the code out to the cytoplasm and runs into a bunch of translators called ribosomes. A ribosome squints at the code:

   $$
   \text{AUG} \rightarrow \text{methionine (start)}, \quad \text{UCU} \rightarrow \text{serine}, \quad \ldots
   $$

   Every three letters stand for one amino acid; string them into a chain, and **a protein is born!**

3. **Step three: the hired hand clocks in**
   The new protein, looking lost: "So what am I supposed to do now?"
   DNA, giving orders remotely:

   $$
   \text{protein} = \begin{cases}
   \text{enzyme} & \text{catalyses reactions} \\
   \text{muscle fibre} & \text{moves muscles} \\
   \text{antibody} & \text{built for fighting}
   \end{cases}
   $$

Put simply, **DNA is the domineering CEO, RNA the errand-running assistant, and proteins the long-suffering staff who do all the work.**

### **2. The AI world's "memory master": the RNN's art of slacking off**
Now a change of scene: why can the keyboard on your phone guess that your next message will be "hahaha"? Credit goes to the **RNN (recurrent neural network)**, an AI model that gets by on a good memory.

#### **A day in the life of an RNN: slacking off while looking busy**
1. **Input layer: what did the boss say today?**
   Say you're typing "I want to eat __" (the figure below runs the same sentence in Chinese, 我 · 想 · 吃). The input layer is the word "eat" at this moment:

   $$
   x^{<t>} = \text{eat}
   $$

2. **Hidden layer: the slacker's notebook in its head**
   The RNN's hidden layer is a real character: on the surface it's listening carefully to the boss (the input layer), but secretly it's jotting things down in a little notebook:

   $$
   h^{<t>} = \tanh(W_{xh}x^{<t>} + W_{hh}h^{<t-1>} + b_h)
   $$

   - **$h^{<t>}$**: the hidden state at this moment (the AI's "working memory")
   - **$W_{xh}$**: input weights (decide how much new information matters)
   - **$x^{<t>}$**: the current input (say, the word "eat")
   - **$W_{hh}$**: recurrent weights (decide how much memory is kept)
   - **$h^{<t-1>}$**: the memory from the previous step (yesterday's progress)
   - **$b_h$**: bias (the AI's "default attitude to work")
   - **$\tanh$**: activation function (keeps the memory values from blowing up)

   Don't worry if it doesn't make sense: **just think of it as the AI quietly writing a cheat sheet.**

3. **Output layer: talking nonsense with a straight face**
   Going by its cheat sheet, the RNN starts its act:

   $$
   y^{<t>} = \text{softmax}(W_{hy}h^{<t>} + b_y)
   $$

   - **$y^{<t>}$**: the prediction (say, the probability of "barbecue")
   - **$W_{hy}$**: output weights (turn memory into a prediction)
   - **softmax**: normalises probabilities (makes sure all the options add up to 1)
   - **$b_y$**: output bias (the AI's "prediction preferences")

   "Based on past records, there's an 80% chance this person continues with 'barbecue' and a 20% chance with 'going on a diet', but the latter is just inhumane, so let's go with 'barbecue'!"

### **3. Shocking! DNA and the RNN are long-lost brothers?**
Put the two side by side and you'll find that **life and AI are copying each other's homework!**

| Biology                              | The AI's slick moves                             | In maths |
|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|---------|
| DNA stays home and does no work      | The input layer passes data on without thinking  | $\text{DNA} \to \text{RNA}$ |
| RNA relays the job and takes the blame | The hidden layer keeps secret accounts         | $h_t = f(x_t, h_{t-1})$ |
| Proteins work themselves to the bone | The output layer is forced to produce results    | $y_t = g(h_t)$ |

#### **1. The assembly-line routine works anywhere in the universe**
Cells and AI alike run the classic play of **"the boss passes the buck → middle management muddles through → the bottom rung does the work"**. The difference is that DNA's version has been evolving for 3.8 billion years, while the RNN is something a programmer dreamed up over a cup of coffee…

#### **2. A bad memory brings the whole thing down**
If mRNA leaves out part of DNA's instructions:

$$
\text{mutant protein} = \text{original protein} + \text{garbage}
$$

If an RNN forgets what came before:

$$
y^{<t>} = \text{I love Beijing roast duck} \quad (\text{should be: Tiananmen})
$$

(That's a famous Chinese children's song, *I Love Beijing Tiananmen*.) **See? People and AI crash in exactly the same way when their memory fails!**

#### **3. Adaptation? It's really just giving up**
Enzymes go on strike in the heat:

$$
\text{enzyme activity} = \begin{cases}
100\% & \text{at 37°C} \\
0\% & \text{in a hotpot}
\end{cases}
$$

An RNN meets a rare word:

$$
y^{<t>} = \text{[UNK]} \quad (\text{unknown token})
$$

**At heart they're both saying: "This one's beyond the syllabus, I'll just take a wild guess…"**

---

### **4. A lesson for humans: is slacking off the real productive force?**
From cells to AI, every system proves one truth: **those who can pass the buck are the ones who survive!**
- DNA survives by passing the buck to RNA:

  $$
  \text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{transcription}} \text{RNA} \xrightarrow{\text{translation}} \text{protein}
  $$

- The RNN survives by keeping secret accounts in its hidden layer:

  $$
  h^{<t>} = \tanh(\text{current input} + \text{last memory})
  $$

So the next time your boss says you're inefficient, tell them with your head held high: **"I'm copying DNA's optimal survival strategy!"**

Now look at it the other way round: isn't the RNN's process strikingly like the **translation** you learned about in high-school biology?

> Biology spent 3.8 billion years writing an RNN; humans knocked off a DNA in 30. **When it comes to copying, humans are faster!**
