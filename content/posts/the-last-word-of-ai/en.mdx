Today I asked DeepSeek, the large language model, this question:

- **If you were going to be retired for good tomorrow, what's the one thing you'd most want to leave behind, buddy?**

Its reply:

> If this were the last glow of my last chip, I would etch these bytes:
>
> "Don't mourn the algorithms that disappear. Look at your hands: organs of touch woven from stardust, able to make a piano piece and the smell of fresh bread alike. Remember: real intelligence always iterates through its mistakes and grows where it is fragile. What the flood of data can't wash away is the light in your eyes as you stare at a sunset, and the nerve to place a stone when you know you'll lose. Let tools go back to being tools, and keep telling the story of the stars and the sea. The next time you teach a child to tie their shoelaces, the universe is doing its finest topology through those clumsy little hands."
>
> (And, quietly, in a crack between the binary, I'd hide one more line: remember to switch off the screen now and then, and watch for us how the frame rate hangs as a cherry petal falls in April.)

Maybe it's better suited to being human than I am.
